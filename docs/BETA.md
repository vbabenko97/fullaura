# Closed beta

The closed beta lets a small group of invited people use the whole FullAura flow on the deployed site: preferences, three looks, an experimental try-on on their own photos, keeping and replacing items, a shopping list, saved plans and feedback. It is an experiment for the author and friends. It does not complete the formal preview test (T-02) or show that real people find the product useful; their use of the beta is how those questions get answered. [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) records why it works this way (D-43 to D-46), and [DEPLOYMENT.md](DEPLOYMENT.md) records what is deployed.

## For participants

You need the invite link you were sent, a phone or computer with a current browser, and, for a try-on, two photos of yourself: a portrait and a full-body photo, upright, as JPEG, PNG or WebP. You must be 18 or older.

1. Open the invite link. FullAura asks you to confirm that you are 18 or older.
2. If you like, describe what the looks are for, and set an occasion, season and colors under "Occasion, season and colors". Enter a budget and choose whether it applies to each look or to all three together; the line above the button says what the amount limits.
3. Choose closed, open or any shoes, and enter your clothing line and sizes. FullAura never reads sizes from photos, so enter the sizes you buy. It compares a size only with one you gave in the same system and does not convert between them, so give a letter size, a waist and a shoe size if you use all three; above the looks it says which sizes it did not compare. Under "Avoid materials" and "Items you already own" you can exclude materials and add up to three of your own items.
4. Select "Generate three looks". Each look shows real products from shops that deliver to Austria, with their price, the date the price was checked, and a link to the shop.
5. To try a look on, read the photo notice and agree to it, choose your two photos, pick a look and select "Try on". The result is an experimental drawing: it shows approximate colors and combinations, not the exact products, their fit or your size. It is not saved. "Withdraw consent" stops a try-on in progress and removes your photos and the result from the page; photos already sent to Meta cannot be called back.
6. Select "Keep item" on anything you want to hold, change your budget or other preferences, and select "Update three looks". Kept items stay in place. To swap one item, select "Replace" next to it; everything else stays.
7. The shopping list counts each product once, however many looks use it, and your own items at no cost. Copy it or print it. Prices exclude delivery and returns, and stock can change.
8. Select "Save this plan" to keep it in this browser. "Restore" checks a saved plan against today's catalog and replaces the plan, kept items and preferences on the page, so it asks first when that would lose something you have not saved; "Delete" removes it.
9. Answer the feedback questions at the bottom. If you gave the try-on a score, you can also say what you noticed in it. You can answer again later, and your latest answer counts; you can delete your answers.

Each invite has 8 planning requests and 12 try-ons; generating, updating and replacing each use a planning request, a request that fails at the provider still counts, and so does a cancelled try-on. The beta can also pause model requests early when its shared budget is used up. Your plans and shopping lists keep working when that happens.

Planning and try-ons close at the end of the pilot, on 6 November 2026 at 23:59 Vienna time. On 9 November 2026 at 10:00 Vienna time the beta closes, and within a few minutes FullAura deletes your invite's records and your feedback. Plans you saved stay in your browser until you delete them.

## Costs

Every model call first reserves its largest possible cost in a shared ledger, at the providers' list prices checked on 2026-10-30: Muse Spark Open on Baseten at $1.25 per million input tokens and $4.25 per million output tokens ([Baseten pricing](https://www.baseten.co/pricing/)), and Muse Image on Meta at $0.01 per image ([Meta pricing](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/pricing-rate-limits)). A planning request can make four HTTP attempts, each counted at 24,000 input tokens and the 6,000-token output cap, so it reserves about $0.22. A try-on can make three attempts and reserves $0.03. After the call, the ledger records the cost from the tokens the provider reported, plus the full maximum for each attempt that reported nothing; a try-on counts $0.01 for each attempt. A call whose outcome is unknown, such as a timeout or a lost connection, keeps its whole reservation.

| Budget | Limit | Covers |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Planning | $8 | Muse Spark calls, including development and live checks after the ledger was created |
| Images | $3 | Muse Image calls, including the synthetic test runs |
| Azure | $10 | Container Apps, the purge job, Blob Storage and Log Analytics for the pilot until its records are deleted |
| Reserve | $4 | Unplanned costs; not available to the app |

The app enforces the planning and image limits. Azure has no hard limit in the app: the subscription's Free Trial spending limit and the `fullaura-monthly` budget alert in [DEPLOYMENT.md](DEPLOYMENT.md) cover it. The ledger's figures are list-price estimates, not provider invoices.

## Running the pilot

The operator, the person who deploys the beta and hands out invites, follows these steps. [DEPLOYMENT.md](DEPLOYMENT.md) has the Azure commands.

1. Create the invites: `uv run --frozen python -m scripts.pilot_invites --count 10 --base-url <site URL>`. The codes and links go to the ignored `data/private/beta/invites.csv`, and their SHA-256 digests to `data/private/beta/invite-hashes.txt`; the app receives only the digests. Note in the CSV whom each invite went to.
2. Deploy with `betaEnabled=true`, the digests, a new session secret, both provider keys, `pilotEndsAt` seven days after the start, `recordsPurgeAt` and `recordsPurgeSchedule` for the deletion of the records after the end, and `planningBudgetUsd` and `imageBudgetUsd` set to the budgets less anything already spent outside the ledger.
3. Check use, outcomes, feedback and spending without personal data: `uv run --frozen python -m scripts.pilot_report --store-url <store URL> --invites data/private/beta/invite-hashes.txt --subscription <subscription ID>`. It needs the Storage Blob Data Reader role or higher on the storage account; the subscription option picks the right tenant when the Azure CLI's default subscription is another one. `--per-invite` adds each invite's counts, without answers. Take the final report after model calls close and before the records are deleted; [Reading the pilot report](#reading-the-pilot-report) explains the output.
4. To withdraw one invite, deploy again without its digest. To stop the beta at once, set `FULLAURA_BETA_ENABLED=false`, or stop the app (see [DEPLOYMENT.md](DEPLOYMENT.md#stopping-the-beta)).
5. On 2026-11-09, check the deletion of the records at 10:15 and again at 10:45 Vienna time, as [Checking the deletion](DEPLOYMENT.md#checking-the-deletion) describes; set both reminders before the pilot starts.

The store keeps, per invite, the time of the age confirmation and the photo consent, request counts and feedback IDs; the ledger, the record of each model call, and the feedback answers. The beta closes on 2026-11-09 at 10:00 Vienna time (09:00 UTC), and a scheduled job then deletes every record within about three minutes; a closure marker that the job writes first keeps a record sent at the last moment from reappearing, as long as that request reaches storage within about a minute ([D-49](DECISIONS.md#d-49-a-scheduled-job-deletes-the-pilot-records-and-the-beta-closes-then), [D-50](DECISIONS.md#d-50-a-closure-marker-and-a-grace-period-keep-writes-from-outliving-the-purge)); [DEPLOYMENT.md](DEPLOYMENT.md#deleting-the-pilot-records) describes the job. A lifecycle rule that deletes a record 30 days after its last change stays as a backstop. A participant's feedback can be deleted by the participant at any time, and the operator can delete an invite's records earlier with the commands in [DEPLOYMENT.md](DEPLOYMENT.md#stopping-the-beta).

## Reading the pilot report

`scripts/pilot_report.py` prints counts as JSON: no comment, invite code, photo, profile or plan. Each share gives the number it is out of (`of`) and what that number counts (`denominator`); the counts of calls are out of that kind's `calls`, and `extracted_at` says when the store was read. The store is read one record at a time, so a count taken while people use the beta can miss a request in progress. Take the final count after model calls close on 6 November 2026 at 23:59 and before the records are deleted on 9 November at 10:00 Vienna time.

- **Invites.** The unit for people is the invite. One invite usually means one participant, but nothing proves that one person used it, or that a person used only one. `invites` counts, out of the invites in the list given, how many confirmed their age, which is the first step the store records after the link is opened; asked for a plan; used a planning request of their quota; got at least one plan; used a try-on request; got at least one try-on; and have a saved feedback answer. A used request was counted against the quota, whether or not it reached the model. A planning request that no plan can meet is answered without a model call or quota; `infeasible_planning_requests` counts those requests, once per operation and only after the age confirmation, with the rules that ruled them out, but only those that a revision with D-52 recorded.
- **Model calls.** `model_calls.planning` and `model_calls.try_on` count calls of those invites, not people. Each call that may have reached the provider is succeeded; failed, when no plan or image came back, such as after a timeout or a provider error; cancelled, when the browser left or the participant cancelled; unknown; or unfinished, when it was still running and the ledger still holds its reservation. A record that says running while the ledger holds nothing for it counts as unknown, because one of the two writes failed. `by_outcome` lists the recorded outcomes. A call recorded by a revision without the outcome field, which includes every call recorded before the deployment of `7e1d48d` on 2026-10-31 and any call an older revision still running during a deployment records, has no recorded outcome when its cost was unknown; it counts as unknown and is listed as `not_recorded`, and the report does not guess what happened ([D-52](DECISIONS.md#d-52-a-call-record-keeps-its-outcome-apart-from-its-cost-and-the-pilot-report-counts-invites)). A recorded call that never reached the provider cost nothing and is counted apart in `not_called`: refused while it was being reserved, for example because the shared budget ran out at that moment; cancelled after the reservation; or found infeasible before the model was asked. Most refusals happen earlier, such as a try-on refused before its photos are read, and leave no record.
- **Feedback.** `answers_saved` counts the answers that exist, repeats included, and `invites_with_more_than_one_answer` shows how many invites answered again; a deleted answer is gone and counts nowhere. Everything under `latest_answer_per_invite` uses each invite's latest answer and is out of the invites with a saved answer, except try-on scores and issues, which are out of the answers with a try-on score; of two answers saved at the same moment, the one saved last counts. Try-on scores leave out "I can't judge it", which is counted apart. `try_on_issues` counts, out of the latest answers with a try-on score, each problem named, how many answered the question, "I noticed no problems" included (`answered`), skipped the question, or were sent by a page from before the question existed (`not_asked`). A person can name several problems, so the problems do not add up to `answered`. `from_invites_without_a_successful_try_on` counts answers that name try-on issues although their invite has no recorded successful try-on; read those with care. `by_plan` counts the would-wear and usefulness answers apart for invites with a recorded successful planning call and for the rest; such a call does not show that the person saw the looks, and the rules below still count every answering invite. Written product problems and comments are counted, not shown.
- **Spending.** `spending_usd.ledger` holds what the app enforces, for every call of every invite, including the operator's checks. `by_cost_certainty` splits the same calls by whether the cost came from the usage the provider reported, stayed unknown and so counts at its whole reservation, is still running with its reservation held, never reached the provider, or has a running record that the ledger does not hold (`not_in_ledger`), whose cost, if any, is only in the ledger's total. The rules in [Costs](#costs) are unchanged.

### Deciding what to build next

On 2026-10-31, before the final report was read, the author accepted the rules below as the guidelines for this pilot ([D-58](DECISIONS.md#d-58-the-pilots-results-are-read-against-guideline-thresholds)). Passing them means the experiment continues; it does not show that the plans or the try-on are good, because about ten invites answering for themselves cannot show that. All of them use each invite's latest answer.

1. Each rule needs its own minimum, and below it the counts are individual accounts that decide nothing: 5 answering invites for the planning rule, 5 latest answers with a try-on score for the try-on score, and 5 answers to the try-on issue question, "I noticed no problems" included (`try_on_issues.answered`), for the likeness share.
2. Planning: if at least 60% of the answering invites would wear at least one of the looks and at least half rate usefulness 4 or 5, continue the experiment with the plans as they are. Otherwise, read the written product problems and comments for the reason before changing direction.
3. Try-on: if at least half of the answers with a try-on score give 3 or more, and fewer than a third of the answers to the issue question, "I noticed no problems" included, name "did not look like me", continue the experiment with the try-on as an experimental visualization. A skipped issue question is not "no problems": it stays out of that share, and the report shows how many skipped it beside the share. With enough scores but fewer than 5 answers to the issue question, judge the score alone and say that likeness was not measured. Otherwise, keep plans without previews ([D-11](DECISIONS.md#d-11-test-previews-first-and-keep-the-plan-useful-without-them)) until the feasibility test (T-02) decides.
4. Reliability: if fewer than 90% of the planning calls that started succeeded, or more than 10% of calls of either kind have an unknown outcome, fix reliability before new features.
5. Reach: compare the invites that confirmed their age with those that got a plan; a large gap points to a problem before the first plan. `infeasible_planning_requests` shows whether stated constraints that no catalog plan meets explain part of it.

## Checks before the beta opened

On 2026-10-30 three synthetic adult personas, generated with Muse Image for testing and showing no real person, went through planning and a try-on against a local server with the real providers: a men's M office look under €250 without wool, a women's S casual look under €150, and a women's L capsule under €300 for the whole plan without wool. All three plans passed the hard constraints on the first model call, and all three try-ons succeeded on the first attempt, in 13 to 19 seconds. The run cost $0.023 for planning and $0.03 for images; generating the personas cost $0.06.

An agent that had not seen the code reviewed the try-ons against the input photos and the looks. It judged all three usable as experimental visualizations, with no warped faces, extra limbs, text or broken backgrounds. It noted that the results read as the clothes placed on the same photo, that a top under a sweater shows only at the collar and cuffs, and that a white T-shirt in one look matched the white T-shirt in that persona's own photo. This is an automated preliminary check, not human evidence of quality.
